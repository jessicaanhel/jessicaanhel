Our wedding website, and the planning panel behind it.

I built both for our own wedding in Tuscany: a phone-first invitation for the guests, and a private panel where the couple and their helpers plan the day. Here is what is inside, and how you can reuse it.

Two products, one project

The invitation is what the guests see: an envelope that opens, our story, the schedule, the map, where to stay and an RSVP form. It is in English and Ukrainian, and it is a phone-width column on any screen.

The panel is what we use to plan. It has tasks, budget, vendors, guests, inspiration boards, and a private board for each team. Everyone logs in with their own account and sees only what their role allows.

  • 2 languages on the invitation
  • 5 roles in the panel
  • 0 database servers: the data lives in Google Sheets

Small things we needed along the way

Important tasks in the calendar
A star on a task is enough. Its deadline gets a red number in the calendar, and the number counts how many important things are due that day.
Prices per person
Catering is not one price, it is a price times the guests. Set the number of guests once at the top and every per-person item follows it, also when the guest list changes.
A short list on the first screen
The Overview shows the next five tasks and a button for five more. The Tasks page shows the next three months, always with the overdue ones, in pages.
A panel you can hide
The calendar and history sit on the right on a computer and can be hidden. On a phone they move to the Overview.

    Decisions I made

    The text is not in the code
    Words, dates and photos live in two content files. Making this site for another couple means changing text, not components.
    A phone-width column on every screen
    The layout uses container queries, so the invitation looks like a phone also on a laptop, and nothing gets lost in a wide window.
    Google Sheets as the database
    The couple can open the data and fix a row by hand. New columns are only added at the end, so old and new code can run together during a deploy without breaking the sheet.
    One list of rules for who can do what
    The menu and the server read the same permission keys. A role that cannot open a tab gets a 403 from the API, not just a hidden button.
    A board the site admin cannot open
    Permissions are the bride's to change, except one. The Groom Board is excluded on purpose, and it is not even on the Permissions page. A surprise has to survive the person who runs the site.
    Shared lists that feel instant
    Changes show at once and are confirmed by the server. Lists refresh by themselves, removed items wait seven days in a trash, and "are you sure" questions are in the page, not browser pop-ups.
    Honest about what it cannot do
    Files are links (Drive, Dropbox), because a sheet cell cannot hold a file. A note can be sent to Apple Notes as a copy, and I call it a copy, not a sync.

    Built with Next.js, TypeScript, Google Sheets and Google's API. Passwords are hashed, sessions are signed cookies.
